Hearth Features Privacy Self-host GitHub

Privacy policy

For the Hearth app on iOS and Android. Effective 13 September 2026.

The short version. Hearth is self-hosted. The app sends your account, your location and your family's data to one place only: the Hearth server you choose to sign in to. That server is run by you, your family, or someone you trust. It is not run by the people who make Hearth, and we cannot see anything on it. There are no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking SDKs in the app.

Who this policy is from

Hearth is open-source software published by the Hearth project (github.com/Qureshi-DH/hearth). We publish the app and the server software. We do not run a Hearth service, and we do not receive, store or process the data the app handles.

Who is responsible for your data

Whoever operates the server you connect to. If you set up Hearth for your family, that is you. If someone else did, that is them, and they decide how long history is kept, who can sign up and where backups go. Questions about data held on a server, and requests to see, correct or delete it, go to its operator. The app's own controls, described below, cover most of them.

What the app sends to your server

Data Why
Email address, display name and password To create and sign in to your account. The server stores the password as a hash.
Profile photo, if you add one So your circle recognises you. The app re-encodes the photo before uploading, which removes the location and camera details it may contain.
Location: position, accuracy, speed, heading, altitude, and whether you are still, walking, cycling or driving To show your circle where you are, notice when you arrive at or leave a place, record trips, and raise SOS and crash alerts.
Battery level and whether the phone is charging So your circle can tell a flat battery from a phone that is simply quiet.
Device name, platform, app and system version, a notification token, and the IP address the device last connected from To list your signed-in devices, deliver notifications to them, and help the operator spot a sign-in that isn't yours.
What you create: circles, places, check-ins, SOS alerts and quick messages Because those are the features you are using.

By default a server keeps location history for 30 days per circle, and never more than 90, though its operator can change both. Your current position is kept until the next one replaces it.

Location in the background

While sharing is on, Hearth collects your location even when the app is closed or not in use. That is what lets your family see you without you opening the app, and what makes arrival alerts, trips, SOS and crash detection work. When you have stopped somewhere, the app switches to a low-power mode and reports rarely. You can pause sharing, share only an approximate area (about 750 metres), or turn sharing off at any time, separately for each circle.

Services the app talks to besides your server

  • Notifications. To receive notifications, the phone registers with Apple Push Notification service or Firebase Cloud Messaging. Depending on how your server is set up, notifications are relayed through Expo's push service or through an ntfy server your operator runs. A notification carries names and a short line of text, such as "Maya arrived at School". It never contains coordinates.
  • Maps. Map images are downloaded from the map provider your server is configured with, OpenFreeMap by default. Like any map, the provider can see which area you are looking at and your IP address.
  • Street names. To show "near Valencia St", the app asks the phone's own geocoding service, Apple's on iPhone and Google's on Android, which receives the coordinates being looked up.
  • App stores. Apple and Google collect data about downloads and purchases under their own policies.

That is the complete list. Nothing else leaves your phone.

What stays on your phone

Your settings, a short queue of locations waiting to upload if you are offline, and a diagnostic log of what the tracker did. The log never leaves the phone unless you choose to share it from You, Tracker diagnostics. Your sign-in tokens are kept in the phone's secure keychain.

Permissions

Location, including background location. Motion and fitness (iPhone) or physical activity (Android), so the app knows when you are still and can save battery. Notifications. The camera, only when you scan an invite QR code. Your photos, only through the system picker, which hands the app the one image you choose. On Android, an exemption from battery optimisation so the phone does not stop sharing in the background. Hearth never asks for your contacts, microphone, Bluetooth or advertising identifier.

Crash detection reads the accelerometer, gyroscope and barometer during a drive. Those readings are worked through on the phone and are not uploaded. Only the alert is, if a crash is detected and you don't cancel it.

Your choices

  • Pause, approximate or stop sharing in You, Location sharing.
  • Download everything your server holds about you in You, Privacy & data, Export my data.
  • Erase your location history in You, Privacy & data, Erase my location history.
  • Delete your account in You, Privacy & data, Delete my account. See deleting your account for exactly what that removes.

Children

Hearth is made for families, and families often use it to keep track of children. We do not collect data from anyone, children included. Accounts for children are created on the family's own server by the adults who run it. Anyone in a circle has the same controls as everyone else, including pausing and leaving, unless a circle admin has turned pausing off, which every member can see.

Security

The store versions of the app connect to servers on the internet only over HTTPS. Plain HTTP is allowed only for a server on your own local network. How the server itself is secured and backed up is up to its operator.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new effective date, and the history of every change is public in the project's repository.

Contact

For questions about this policy or the app, open an issue at github.com/Qureshi-DH/hearth/issues. For questions about the data on a particular server, contact the person who runs it.
